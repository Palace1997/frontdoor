What To Expect At A First Appointment

Not knowing what happens in the room is one of the reasons people put off calling. Here is the shape of a first appointment, start to finish.

Dana Whitfield, LADC·Clinical Director·November 6, 2026

If you have never done this before, the first appointment is the part that is hardest to picture. It helps to know that it is mostly a conversation, that you are not expected to have your thoughts in order, and that nothing is decided before you are ready.

Before You Come In

We send the paperwork ahead of time so you are not filling in forms in a waiting room. It asks for your contact details, your insurance if you are using it, and who we should call in an emergency. Bring a photo ID and your insurance card if you have one. If you are meeting by video, test the link the day before so a login is not the first thing you deal with.

The First Conversation

Most of the first appointment is listening. You will be asked what brought you in, what you have already tried, and what you would like to be different. There is no right way to answer. If a question is one you would rather not take on yet, say so and we move past it. You set the pace, in the first appointment and after it.

What You Decide Together

By the end you should leave with a plan you understand: how often you would meet, roughly what the work would focus on, and what it costs. None of that is locked in. People change direction once they get going, and bringing that up is part of the work rather than an interruption to it.

If It Is Not The Right Fit

Sometimes it is not, and that is worth saying out loud rather than quietly not booking again. Tell us and we will help you look elsewhere. Finding the right person matters more than filling our calendar, and we would rather make an introduction than lose you to a bad first try.

If a question is in the way of booking, call or send a message and a person will answer. Please keep health details out of the form, we will cover those with you in the appointment.
